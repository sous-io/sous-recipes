## Rulings and Provenance

- A ruling is a decision a person made directly, or one recorded in an accepted decision record;
  anything else, including anything an agent concluded, is an inference.
- When writing a rule or a decision anywhere that lasts, make clear which it is. In a task file, a
  skill, a skill's reference file or a reply, quote a ruling's exact words, with an attribution line
  under the quote; in a memory, point at where the ruling is recorded (a short quote MAY follow).
- Mark an inference as one: "(Agent suggestion; the user has not ruled on it.)".
- When reading, treat a rule that neither quotes nor points at a ruling or a decision record as
  tentative: follow it by default, and ask the user when it blocks the work or conflicts with
  something else.
- Nothing is law forever: a ruling changes through a newer ruling or a newer decision record.

The full rule, with examples, is the always-loaded "Rulings and Provenance" memory of the
`communication/agent-conduct` recipe.
